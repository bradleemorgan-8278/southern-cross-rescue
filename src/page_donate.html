Fund 01

General Operations Fund

Rescue work costs far more than most people realize, and most of it happens before a dog is ever posted for adoption. This fund covers the real, ongoing costs of running Southern Cross day to day.

  • Travel to evaluate every dog we consider taking in, and to visit potential foster and adoptive homes in person
  • In-person temperament testing for every dog at intake — critical to placing them with the right handler, and to catching hidden training or medical needs early
  • Food, supplies, and veterinary care, covered in full for every dog in our program
  • Foster support — crates, bedding, and equipment for every foster home

100% of this fund goes directly to the dogs. Southern Cross currently pays no staff salaries — every hour given by our team is volunteered. Paid staff is a long-term goal as we grow, but today, your gift funds dogs, not overhead.

Fund 02

The Tommy Legacy Fund

Tommy arrived at Southern Cross emaciated, heartworm positive, and suffering from severe elbow dysplasia. It took ten months of treatment before he was healthy enough to be adopted — but by then, foster mom Laura knew he wasn't leaving. For nearly twelve years, he became her partner in rescue, teaching frightened dogs and puppies alike that they were finally safe.

This fund carries his work forward — covering medical care, behavioral rehabilitation, and extended foster support for German Shepherds whose needs exceed what standard rescue funding can cover. Some dogs need surgery. Some need months, not weeks, to learn to trust again. Tommy was one of those dogs. Because of him, others will get the same chance.

Fund 03

The Shepherds' Circle

Emergencies don't wait for a fundraiser. The Shepherds' Circle is our monthly giving community — donors whose recurring gifts provide the dependable, predictable support that lets us say "yes" the moment a German Shepherd needs us most.

A steady monthly gift, even a small one, does something a one-time gift can't: it lets us plan ahead instead of reacting one crisis at a time.
